## 二、英文正文

### 3.1.3 Dynamic Covalent Bonds

In conventional molecular systems, covalent bonds maintain the molecular skeleton, whereas non-covalent interactions govern higher-order organization. Amphiphilic molecules illustrate this division: robust covalent linkages connect hydrophilic and hydrophobic segments, while hydrogen bonding and other non-covalent interactions control their supramolecular assembly [52]. Because non-covalent interactions are reversible, they support molecular recognition, recognition-directed organization and self-healing. Dynamic covalent chemistry (DCC) extends this design logic to covalent networks by introducing reversible bond cleavage and recombination. Covalent connectivity can therefore be retained while the network reorganizes in response to chemical or physical conditions [53]. Wang et al. demonstrated this principle by constructing an azobenzene–galactopyranoside supra-amphiphile through dynamic covalent bond formation, which exhibited dual-responsive assembly behavior [52]. In such systems, the assembled structure reflects the interplay between dynamic covalent exchange and the non-covalent interactions among the resulting amphiphilic species.

Representative dynamic covalent motifs include reversible Diels–Alder adducts, Schiff bases, disulfide bonds, boronate ester bonds and dynamic thiol–ene exchange [54–59]. These chemistries have been incorporated into self-healing hydrogels and related adaptive networks. After mechanical damage, broken dynamic covalent bonds can reconnect through reversible reactions. Hydrogen bonding, electrostatic interactions and metal–ligand coordination can simultaneously facilitate local conformational rearrangement. The combined processes enable repair across microscopic and macroscopic length scales. However, recovery after damage does not by itself establish that the initial network formed through self-assembly. The formation pathway and the role of each interaction must therefore be distinguished.

Extending this dissipation-and-repair mechanism to electrochemical energy storage offers a route to mitigate structural failure in high-specific-energy electrodes subject to large electrochemically induced volume changes. To limit the extreme swelling of high-capacity anodes, Lin et al. developed an energy-dissipative crosslinked binder, termed TCB, from tannic acid (TA), carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) and boric acid (BA) [60]. The binder contains multidimensional hydrogen bonds and dynamic boronate ester bonds. During repeated silicon expansion and contraction, the hydrogen bonds rupture preferentially as sacrificial bonds, absorbing and dissipating mechanical stress. Meanwhile, the dynamic boronate ester bonds form a three-dimensional framework that cushions volume expansion and undergoes repeated cleavage and recombination. This exchange allows network reconfiguration during galvanostatic cycling and, under the reported conditions, helps maintain electrical contact among active particles. The cooperative network therefore supports electrode structural integrity, although the electrochemical response also depends on binder distribution, electrode architecture and testing conditions.

### 3.2 External Field-Induced Self-Assembly

External physical fields, including optical, electric and magnetic fields, can modulate local interactions among building blocks and alter the effective energy landscape and kinetic pathways of assembly [61,62]. By coupling to photoresponsive states, charge or dipole distributions, or magnetic moments, a field can change particle or molecular orientation, interparticle attraction and activation barriers. The resulting structure is not necessarily the global minimum of the field-free free-energy landscape. Depending on relaxation kinetics and field persistence, the structure may approach an equilibrium state, remain metastable or become kinetically trapped. It may also persist only while the field is applied or disassemble after the input is removed. Thus, the structure and lifetime of a field-induced assembly depend on both the applied field and its response pathway.

### 3.2.1 Optical Field Induction

Optical-field-induced self-assembly relies mainly on photochemical or photophysical processes that alter the conformation, polarity or intermolecular affinity of photoresponsive molecules [63]. Azobenzene is widely used because light-driven trans–cis isomerization changes molecular geometry and can perturb the interactions that stabilize supramolecular structures. Zhu et al. incorporated azobenzene derivatives, disperse red 1 (DR1) and 4-aminoazobenzene (Aazo), into a poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) matrix. They then combined PAA with poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO) to fabricate hydrogen-bonded polymer fibers [64]. Upon photoexcitation, the azobenzene groups underwent trans–cis isomerization. This conformational transition drove rearrangement of the fibrous supramolecular architecture and increased both the initial modulus and yield stress of the material [64]. The study therefore links a defined molecular switch to a mesoscale structural and mechanical response. The extent of reorganization depends on hydrogen-bond reversibility and polymer mobility, which may become limiting as liquid content decreases or mechanical confinement increases.

At a different scale, photoinduced electron transfer (PET) provides a light-driven mechanism for energy conversion and information transduction. PET involves directional electron transfer from an electron-rich donor to an electron-deficient acceptor after photoexcitation. Huang et al. investigated conformational folding in a PET system involving a fluorophore–spacer–acceptor configuration [65]. Light-induced folding changed the system from an open to a folded state, shortened the donor–acceptor distance and enhanced electron-transfer efficiency. This study provides evidence for molecular-scale conformational regulation coupled to PET. Without additional evidence for aggregates or long-range order, it should not be treated as direct evidence of mesoscale or long-range self-assembly [65].

## 三、中文对照

### 3.1.3 动态共价键

在传统分子体系中，共价键负责维持分子骨架，而非共价作用则主导更高层级的结构组织。两亲分子体现了这种分工：稳定的共价连接将亲水链段与疏水链段连接起来，而氢键及其他非共价作用控制其超分子组装行为[52]。由于非共价作用具有可逆性，它们能够支持分子识别、识别导向的组织以及自修复。动态共价化学（DCC）将这一设计逻辑拓展到共价网络中，通过引入可逆的共价键断裂与重组，使共价连接得以保留，同时允许网络响应化学或物理条件而发生重排[53]。Wang 等通过动态共价成键构筑了偶氮苯–半乳糖吡喃糖苷超两亲分子，并观察到其具有双重响应的组装行为[52]。在这类体系中，组装结构由动态共价交换与所得两亲性组分之间的非共价作用共同决定。

具有代表性的动态共价基元包括可逆 Diels–Alder 加合物、Schiff 碱、二硫键、硼酸酯键和动态 thiol–ene 交换[54–59]。这些化学机制已被用于自修复水凝胶及相关自适应网络。机械损伤发生后，断裂的动态共价键可以通过可逆反应重新连接。与此同时，氢键、静电作用和金属–配体配位能够促进局部构象重排。这些过程协同作用，使结构能够在微观和宏观尺度上实现修复。不过，损伤后的恢复本身并不能证明初始网络由自组装形成。因此，需要区分网络的形成路径以及各类相互作用分别发挥的作用。

将这种耗散—修复机制拓展到电化学储能体系，为缓解高比能电极在电化学诱导的大体积变化下发生的结构失效提供了一条途径。为抑制高容量负极的剧烈膨胀，Lin 等以单宁酸（TA）、羧甲基纤维素（CMC）和硼酸（BA）为原料，构筑了称为 TCB 的耗能交联黏结剂[60]。该黏结剂同时含有多维氢键和动态硼酸酯键。在硅负极反复膨胀和收缩的过程中，氢键作为“牺牲键”优先断裂，从而吸收并耗散机械应力。与此同时，动态硼酸酯键构成具有韧性的三维网络，能够缓冲体积膨胀，并反复发生断裂与重组。这种交换过程使网络能够在恒电流循环过程中持续重排；在文献报道的条件下，它有助于维持活性颗粒之间的电接触。因此，该协同网络能够支撑电极结构完整性，但最终电化学响应还取决于黏结剂分布、电极结构和测试条件。

### 3.2 外场诱导的自组装

包括光场、电场和磁场在内的外部物理场能够调节构筑单元之间的局部相互作用，并改变组装过程中的有效能量景观和动力学路径[61,62]。外场分别通过光响应状态、电荷或偶极分布以及磁矩与体系耦合，从而改变颗粒或分子的取向、颗粒间吸引以及组装能垒。外场形成的结构并不必然是无外场自由能景观中的全局最低态。根据弛豫动力学和外场持续时间的不同，体系可能趋近平衡态，保持亚稳态，陷入动力学受限状态，仅在外场存在时维持，或在外场撤除后发生解组装。因此，外场诱导组装体的结构和寿命同时取决于外场本身以及体系响应所经历的动力学路径。

### 3.2.1 光场诱导

光场诱导的自组装主要依赖光响应分子发生的光化学或光物理过程，这些过程能够改变分子的构象、极性或分子间亲和力[63]。偶氮苯被广泛用于此类体系，因为光驱动的 trans–cis 异构化会改变分子几何结构，并扰动稳定超分子结构的相互作用。Zhu 等将偶氮苯衍生物分散红 1（DR1）和 4-氨基偶氮苯（Aazo）引入聚丙烯酸（PAA）基体，随后将 PAA 与聚氧化乙烯（PEO）复合，制备了氢键聚合物纤维[64]。光激发后，偶氮苯基团发生 trans–cis 异构化。这一构象转变驱动纤维超分子结构重排，并使材料的初始模量和屈服应力均得到提高[64]。该研究因此将明确的分子开关与介观尺度的结构和力学响应联系起来。不过，结构重组程度取决于氢键的可逆性和聚合物迁移能力；随着液体含量降低或机械约束增强，这些条件可能成为限制因素。

在另一尺度上，光诱导电子转移（PET）提供了一个用于能量转换和信息传递的光驱动过程。PET 过程涉及光激发后，电子从富电子供体定向转移至缺电子受体。Huang 等研究了一个包含“荧光团–间隔基–受体”构型的 PET 体系中的构象折叠行为[65]。光诱导折叠使体系由开放态转变为折叠态，缩短了供体与受体之间的距离，并提高了电子转移效率。该研究证明了与 PET 耦合的分子尺度构象调控。若缺少聚集体或长程有序结构的额外证据，则不应将其直接视为介观或长程自组装的证据[65]。
