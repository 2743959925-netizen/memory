## 英文整章修改稿

Self-assembly provides a common framework for connecting molecular interactions with battery architectures. The preceding sections show that its role changes across length scales. In electrode structures, it can organize contact and mechanical buffering. In electrolytes, it can reshape solvation or construct ion-transport pathways. At solid-state interfaces, it can regulate contact, interphase chemistry, and local ion flux. These examples support a conditional view of translation from liquid to solid batteries. The relevant design variable is not the interaction alone, but its match with transport mode, mechanical response, and failure mode.

Several barriers still limit this translation because molecular organization does not automatically produce a continuous pathway at the device scale. Strong interactions may slow ion exchange, whereas soft assemblies may lose mechanical integrity. Rigid frameworks may instead increase brittleness or raise interfacial resistance. Future studies therefore need to connect molecular structure, mesoscale morphology, and cell-level behaviour under the same operating conditions. This connection is essential for distinguishing a transferable design principle from a material-specific effect.

For electrolytes, the central challenge is to extend local coordination control into long-range transport without sacrificing mechanical continuity. Hydrogen bonding, ion coordination, host–guest recognition, and controlled phase separation offer different routes. Their usefulness should be judged by coupled measures of ionic conductivity, ion transference number, interfacial stability, and mechanical response. PISA and CDSA may provide additional kinetic control over this translation. For example, CDSA could introduce crystalline domains into a PEO-based electrolyte, while amorphous segments preserve segmental motion for ion transport. This remains a hypothesis that requires direct validation of domain connectivity, crystallinity, ion transport, and dendrite response [80–90, 100–103].

At solid–solid interfaces, the priority is to maintain a contact that is both compliant and ionically connected. Self-assembled monolayers and multilayers may anchor SEI or CEI components, regulate local electric fields, and accommodate changes in electrode morphology. The key challenge is to prevent a protective layer from becoming a transport barrier. Future work should therefore examine interphase growth, charge distribution, and mechanical adaptation together, preferably under operando conditions. This approach may clarify whether dynamic interphases remain effective during extended plating and stripping or repeated volume changes [20, 22, 104–114].

At active electrodes and current collectors, self-assembled monolayers offer a route to regulate early nucleation, adhesion, and local chemical potentials. This function is especially relevant to anode-free batteries, where the current collector must support uniform metal deposition from the first cycle. Lithiophilic or sodiophilic groups may alter local nucleation behaviour, but their effect depends on layer coverage, electronic coupling, and interfacial stability. The next step is to design these variables together rather than treating nucleation chemistry and mechanical contact as separate problems [49, 94–96].

Beyond hydrogen bonding and electrostatic organization, metal–organic coordination, macrocyclic host–guest recognition, halogen bonding, and dynamic covalent chemistry may expand the design space. Metal–organic frameworks and covalent organic frameworks provide ordered pores and chemical sites, but their transfer into solid-state electrolytes or interphases requires control of brittleness, grain-boundary resistance, and chemical compatibility. A useful strategy may be to combine rigid domains with deformable or self-healing phases. The design objective is not maximum order, but sufficient order for transport and sufficient adaptability for cycling [80–90, 100, 101].

At the device level, progress will depend on whether molecular assembly can be integrated with slurry processing, in situ polymerization, electrode densification, and cell assembly. Relevant tests should include realistic areal loading, limited electrolyte content, stack pressure, temperature variation, and long-term cycling. These conditions determine whether an assembled structure remains connected after processing and repeated operation. Standardized descriptors linking interaction strength, domain size, ion-transport pathway, and mechanical response would make comparisons more informative.

Self-assembly should therefore be treated as a platform for conditional, multiscale design. Its promise lies in coordinating chemistry, transport, and mechanics within one architecture. Its limits arise when these functions are not aligned or cannot survive processing and cycling. Future advances may come from testing this alignment across complete battery components, rather than optimizing one interface or one interaction in isolation.

## 中文对照

自组装为连接分子间作用与电池结构提供了统一框架。前文表明，自组装在不同尺度上的作用并不相同。在电极结构中，它可以组织界面接触并提供力学缓冲。在电解质中，它可以重塑溶剂化环境或构建离子传输路径。在固态界面处，它可以调节接触、界面层化学和局部离子通量。这些案例支持一种具有条件性的液态到固态迁移观点。真正需要匹配的并不只是相互作用本身，还包括传输模式、力学响应和失效模式。

这种迁移仍受到多方面限制。分子组织并不会自动形成器件尺度上的连续传输路径。过强的相互作用可能减慢离子交换。过于柔软的组装体可能失去力学完整性。过于刚性的框架则可能增加脆性或界面阻抗。因此，后续研究需要在相同工作条件下，把分子结构、介观形貌和电池尺度行为联系起来。只有这样，才能区分可迁移的设计原则与材料特有的效应。

对于电解质而言，核心问题是把局部配位调控延伸到长程离子传输，同时保持力学连续性。氢键、离子配位、主–客体识别和可控相分离可以提供不同路径。它们的有效性应通过离子电导率、离子迁移数、界面稳定性和力学响应等指标进行协同评价。PISA 和 CDSA 还可能为这一迁移提供额外的动力学控制。例如，CDSA 可以被探索用于在 PEO 基聚合物电解质中引入晶态结构域，同时保留无定形链段的节段运动和离子传输能力。这仍然是一个需要验证的设计假设，必须直接考察结构域连通性、结晶性、离子传输和枝晶响应 [80–90, 100–103]。

对于固–固界面，首要目标是维持同时具有可变形性和离子连通性的接触。自组装单层和多层膜可以用于锚定 SEI 或 CEI 组分、调节局部电场，并适应电极形貌变化。关键问题是避免保护层转变为传输障碍。因此，后续研究应在同一框架下考察界面层生长、电荷分布和力学适配，并尽可能在 operando 条件下进行。这样的研究有助于判断动态界面层能否在长期沉积/剥离或反复体积变化过程中保持作用 [20, 22, 104–114]。

在活性电极和集流体一侧，自组装单层可以用于调节早期成核、界面黏附和局部化学势。这一功能对无负极电池尤其重要，因为集流体需要从首圈开始支持均匀的金属沉积。亲锂或亲钠基团可能改变局部成核行为，但其作用取决于膜层覆盖度、电子耦合和界面稳定性。下一步需要对这些变量进行协同设计，而不是将成核化学与力学接触视为彼此独立的问题 [49, 94–96]。

除氢键和静电组织外，金属–有机配位、大环主–客体识别、卤键和动态共价化学也可能拓展自组装的设计空间。金属–有机框架和共价有机框架能够提供有序孔道和化学作用位点，但将其引入固态电解质或界面层时，仍需同时控制脆性、晶界阻抗和化学相容性。一种可行方向是将刚性结构域与可变形或具有自修复能力的相结合。设计目标不应是追求最高程度的有序性，而应是在保证传输的同时保留足够的循环适应性 [80–90, 100, 101]。

在器件尺度上，未来进展取决于分子组装能否与浆料加工、原位聚合、电极致密化和电池装配相衔接。相关测试应包括具有代表性的面容量、受限电解液用量、堆叠压力、温度变化和长期循环。这些条件能够检验组装结构在加工和反复运行后是否仍保持连通。建立连接相互作用强度、结构域尺寸、离子传输路径和力学响应的标准化描述，有助于提高不同体系之间的可比性。

因此，自组装更适合被视为一种具有条件性的多尺度设计平台。它的价值在于把化学、传输和力学功能协调到同一结构中。它的局限则来自这些功能之间的不匹配，或组装结构无法经受加工和循环。未来的进展可能取决于对完整电池组件中这种匹配关系的系统验证，而不是孤立优化某一个界面或某一种相互作用。
