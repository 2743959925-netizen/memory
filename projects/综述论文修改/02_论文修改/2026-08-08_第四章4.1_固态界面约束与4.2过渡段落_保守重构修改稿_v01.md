## 英文修改稿

Because solid-state electrolytes lack liquid-phase mobility and capillary wetting, electrode/SSE interfaces must retain contact during cycling. At microscopic and nanoscopic scales, loss of phase-boundary continuity can increase interfacial resistance and local shear stress. Self-assembled non-covalent networks or dynamic covalent frameworks may help construct three-dimensional composite electrodes with connected carrier pathways and mechanical compliance. These constraints also motivate examining self-assembly within the electrolyte phase, where ion transport must be organized beyond the electrode interface.
