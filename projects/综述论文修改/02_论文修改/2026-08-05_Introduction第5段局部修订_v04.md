## D. 替换后的英文

Accordingly, self-assembly has been explored in rechargeable metal batteries to regulate electrode–electrolyte interfaces under liquid-electrolyte conditions. On lithium metal, ordered self-assembled monolayers containing carboxyl groups have been used to promote the formation of a LiF-rich solid electrolyte interphase and improve lithium plating–stripping stability [18]. Electric-field-responsive interfacial modifiers can dynamically reorganize near the electrode surface, thereby homogenizing local Li⁺ flux and lithium deposition [19]. In aqueous zinc batteries, multilayer assemblies based on interactions between amino acids and Zn²⁺ have been employed to restructure the electric double layer and form a protective interfacial buffer, mitigating nonuniform zinc deposition and parasitic interfacial reactions [20]. Together, these examples illustrate three principal roles of self-assembly in liquid batteries: directing interphase formation, redistributing ion flux, and stabilizing electrode–electrolyte interfaces. These liquid-phase applications provide the practical basis for exploring whether related self-organization principles can be extended to solid-state battery architectures.
