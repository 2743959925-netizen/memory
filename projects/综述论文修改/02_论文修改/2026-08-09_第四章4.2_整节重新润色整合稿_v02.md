# 第四章 4.2 Electrolyte Architectures: From Local Solvation to Long-Range Ion Transport

## 英文整节修改稿

Liquid and solid electrolytes use different structural features to support ion transport. Liquid electrolytes rely on local solvation structures and transient ion-transport environments. Solid-state electrolytes require connected pathways through the electrolyte matrix. When anions are less mobile, these pathways may increase the transference number of the target ion. Low transport barriers may also support higher ionic conductivity at room temperature. In both phases, transport and interfacial reactions depend on organization from the molecular to the mesoscale. Self-assembly offers a bottom-up route to organize electrolyte components through hydrogen bonding, electrostatic interactions, van der Waals forces, and coordination bonds.

In conventional liquid electrolytes, Brownian motion supports continuous exchange between local solvation shells and the surrounding liquid. Ethylene carbonate/diethyl carbonate (EC/DEC) electrolytes are a common example. The conventional localized high-concentration electrolyte (LHCE) model assumes that the diluent contributes little to the primary solvation shell. Salt–solvent clusters are therefore treated as dispersed within a diluent-rich matrix. This model provides a reference for understanding more localized electrolyte structures formed by self-assembly.

Efaw et al. [97] revisited this picture using a ternary electrolyte based on lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), and tris(2,2,2-trifluoroethyl) orthoformate (TFEO). LiFSI dissolves in DME but has limited solubility in TFEO, whereas DME and TFEO are miscible. DME therefore acts as a solvent and an interfacial component between salt-rich and diluent-rich regions. The resulting system forms micelle-like salt–solvent clusters within a TFEO-rich matrix. This organization increases the local salt concentration and the population of aggregate (AGG⁺) species. The interfacial structure was associated with preferential reduction and improved lithium-metal cycling under the reported conditions.

Cao et al. [98] examined a related LiFSI/DME–TFEO electrolyte. The electron-withdrawing CF₃ groups in TFEO may lower the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of the electrolyte. TFEO contributes little to the primary Li⁺ solvation shell, consistent with an LHCE-like solvation environment. At the interface, this structure evolved into a monolithic SEI with a LiF-rich inner phase. The resulting interphase was associated with more uniform lithium deposition and smaller fluctuations in interfacial resistance. It also reduced lithium pulverization and dead-lithium formation under the reported conditions. Li||NCM811 cells were charged and discharged at 4 C with this electrolyte.

Yao et al. [99] used 15-crown-5 (15-C-5) as an additive in a sodium-ion battery electrolyte. The molecule was selected through theoretical screening of ether compounds. Its ion–dipole interaction with Na⁺ promoted host–guest association and formed [Na(15-C-5)]⁺ in situ. The bulky and rigid complex altered the local coordination environment and could facilitate PF₆⁻ participation in the primary solvation shell. This favored contact ion pairs (CIPs) and reduced the contribution of free diethylene glycol dimethyl ether (DGM) to Na⁺ coordination. The reorganization was associated with faster low-temperature Na⁺ transport, a lower freezing point, and a thin NaF-rich SEI. The additive improved the reported performance of several sodium-ion anodes across a wider temperature range.

Liquid-electrolyte examples show how self-assembly can reorganize local solvation. Solid-state electrolytes require a different structural solution. Their design often focuses on long-range pathways within a mechanically constrained matrix. Polyrotaxane networks, pore-confined covalent organic frameworks (COFs), and block-copolymer microphase separation provide three representative routes.

Ding et al. [100] used intermolecular hydrogen bonding to thread cyclic 18-crown-6 (18C6) onto a quasi-linear poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) chain. A hexamethylene diisocyanate trimer (HDIt) then capped both PEG termini. This process produced a three-dimensional network with a polyrotaxane-type topology. Hydrogen-bond-rich regions may support directional Li⁺ migration, while steric confinement can limit TFSI⁻ motion. The electrolyte reached a Li⁺ transference number of 0.41, compared with 0.25 for the blank control. Li||Li symmetric cells cycled for more than 400 h at 0.1 mA cm⁻² under the reported conditions. The system also showed a room-temperature ionic conductivity of 3.48 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹, compared with 1.12 × 10⁻⁵ S cm⁻¹ without the polyrotaxane units. The reported electrochemical stability window extended to approximately 4.8 V versus Li/Li⁺. LiFePO₄ and LiNi₀.₈Co₀.₁₅Al₀.₀₅O₂ cells showed improved cycling stability in the reported tests.

Nie et al. [101] incorporated a crown-ether-functionalized COF (COF-Py-CE) into a fluorinated poly(vinylidene fluoride-co-hexafluoropropylene) (PVDF-HFP) matrix. Positive electrostatic potential regions and imine linkages in the COF, together with polar C–F bonds in PVDF-HFP, can immobilize TFSI⁻ through electrostatic adsorption and pore confinement. Crown-ether oxygens provide exchangeable coordination sites for Li⁺ migration between adjacent COF layers. The reported 0.224 eV value describes a crown-ether-mediated interlayer hopping barrier, rather than the activation energy of the whole composite. At 30 °C, the electrolyte showed an ionic conductivity of 1.15 × 10⁻³ S cm⁻¹ and a Li⁺ transference number of 0.91. Under the reported conditions, Li||Li symmetric cells cycled for more than 1500 h at 0.5 mA cm⁻² and 0.5 mAh cm⁻². NCM523 cells retained 90% of their capacity after 2450 cycles at 1 C. A 1.2 Ah pouch cell retained approximately 95.65% of its capacity after 100 cycles.

Sharon et al. [102] compared a polystyrene-block-poly(ethylene oxide) (SEO) block-copolymer electrolyte containing LiTFSI with a PEO homopolymer electrolyte. Under suitable compositions, SEO–LiTFSI formed periodic PEO–LiTFSI and polystyrene nanodomains. The PEO-rich domains served as the main ion-conducting regions, while the polystyrene domains provided mechanical support. As the Li/EO ratio increased from 1/12 to 1/5, PEO–LiTFSI conductivity decreased nearly threefold, whereas SEO–LiTFSI conductivity remained approximately constant. Spectroscopy indicated that additional Li⁺ occupied more PEO ether-oxygen sites in the homopolymer. In the block copolymer, the fractions of complexed and uncomplexed EO sites remained approximately constant. Molecular-dynamics simulations further placed associated ions near the domain interfaces. This interfacial sequestration may help preserve the solvation balance within PEO-rich domains as the salt concentration increases. It also suggests that phase boundaries can host excess salt aggregates rather than simply obstruct ion transport.

Taken together, the liquid examples show that self-assembly acts mainly on local solvation and ion association. Micellar networks and host–guest complexes can change the populations of CIPs and ionic aggregates. These changes may influence desolvation, interphase formation, and the electrochemical stability window. Their effects remain dependent on solvent–salt–diluent interactions, electrode surface, and operating conditions.

In solid and quasi-solid electrolytes, self-assembly instead organizes transport pathways across the matrix. The examples above combine molecular confinement, periodic phase separation, and exchangeable coordination sites. These structures may restrict anion motion while preserving routes for Li⁺ migration. Their transport and mechanical functions remain coupled to framework chemistry, morphology, and temperature. Yan et al. [103] developed selectively fluorinated aromatic lithium salts (SFALS) for PEO-based solid polymer electrolytes. Hydrogen bonding between SFALS anions and PEO contributed to a triad-type Li⁺ solvation structure. The reported system altered Li⁺ coordination and interfacial chemistry, and it showed stable cycling under the reported conditions.

Overall, self-assembly links electrolyte architecture with charge-carrier transport across molecular and mesoscale length scales. In liquid systems, it reorganizes local solvation and ion association. In solid and quasi-solid systems, it organizes longer-range pathways and couples ion transport with mechanical support. These functions do not arise from self-assembly alone. They depend on composition, morphology, interfaces, and operating conditions. The next section therefore considers how related design principles can be extended from bulk electrolytes to solid-state electrolyte interfaces.

## 中文对照

液态和固态电解质依赖不同的结构特征实现离子传输。液态电解质依赖局部溶剂化结构和瞬态离子传输环境。固态电解质则需要在电解质基体中建立连续的传输路径。当阴离子迁移受到限制时，这类路径可能提高目标离子的迁移数。较低的传输能垒也可能有助于提高室温离子电导率。在两类体系中，体相传输和界面反应都受到分子到介观尺度结构组织的影响。自组装可以通过氢键、静电作用、范德华力和配位键，从底层组织电解质组分。

在常规液态电解质中，布朗运动使局部溶剂化壳层与周围液体之间持续发生交换。碳酸乙烯酯/碳酸二乙酯（EC/DEC）电解液是常见例子。传统局部高浓度电解液（LHCE）模型通常认为，稀释剂对第一溶剂化壳层的贡献较小。因此，盐–溶剂团簇通常被视为分散在富稀释剂基体中。这一模型为理解自组装形成的更局域化电解液结构提供了参照。

Efaw 等 [97] 以双（氟磺酰）亚胺锂（LiFSI）、1,2-二甲氧基乙烷（DME）和三（2,2,2-三氟乙基）原甲酸酯（TFEO）构建三元电解液，对这一传统认识进行了拓展。LiFSI 可溶于 DME，但在 TFEO 中的溶解度有限，而 DME 与 TFEO 彼此互溶。因此，DME 同时作为溶剂和富盐区域与富稀释剂区域之间的界面组分。所得体系在富 TFEO 基体中形成了类胶束盐–溶剂团簇。这种组织方式提高了局部盐浓度和聚集体（AGG⁺）物种的比例。该界面结构与所报道条件下的优先还原和锂金属循环稳定性相关。

Cao 等 [98] 研究了相关的 LiFSI/DME–TFEO 电解液。TFEO 中的强吸电子 CF₃ 基团可能降低电解液的最高占据分子轨道（HOMO）能级。TFEO 对 Li⁺ 第一溶剂化壳层的贡献较小，与 LHCE 型溶剂化环境相符。在界面处，这种结构进一步演化形成了具有富 LiF 内层的整体式 SEI。所得界面层与更均匀的锂沉积和更小的界面电阻波动相关。在所报道条件下，它还减缓了锂粉化和死锂形成。采用该电解液的 Li||NCM811 电池可以在 4 C 下进行充放电循环。

Yao 等 [99] 将 15-冠-5（15-C-5）用作钠离子电池电解液添加剂。该分子通过醚类化合物理论筛选得到。15-C-5 与 Na⁺ 之间的离子–偶极作用促进了主–客体缔合，并原位形成 [Na(15-C-5)]⁺。该复合物具有较大的体积和较高的刚性，可以改变局部配位环境，并可能促进 PF₆⁻ 参与第一溶剂化壳层。这一变化有利于接触离子对（CIPs）的形成，并减少游离二乙二醇二甲醚（DGM）分子对 Na⁺ 配位的贡献。该结构重组与更快的低温 Na⁺ 传输、更低的凝固点和富 NaF 薄层 SEI 相关。在文献报道的条件下，该添加剂提高了多种钠离子负极在更宽温度范围内的电化学性能。

上述液态电解液案例说明，自组装主要作用于局部溶剂化结构。固态电解质需要不同的结构解决方案。其设计通常聚焦于受力学约束基体中的长程传输路径。聚轮烷网络、孔道限域的共价有机框架（COF）和嵌段共聚物微相分离，代表了三类结构路径。

Ding 等 [100] 利用分子间氢键，将环状 18-冠-6（18C6）穿入准线性聚乙二醇（PEG）链。随后，采用六亚甲基二异氰酸酯三聚体（HDIt）封端 PEG 两端。该过程形成了具有聚轮烷型拓扑结构的三维网络。富含氢键的区域可能支持 Li⁺ 定向迁移，而空间位阻则可以限制 TFSI⁻ 运动。该电解质的 Li⁺ 迁移数达到 0.41，而空白对照为 0.25。在所报道条件下，Li||Li 对称电池在 0.1 mA cm⁻² 下循环超过 400 h。该体系的室温离子电导率为 3.48 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹，而未组装聚轮烷功能单元时为 1.12 × 10⁻⁵ S cm⁻¹。报道的电化学稳定窗口约为 4.8 V（vs Li/Li⁺）。采用 LiFePO₄ 和 LiNi₀.₈Co₀.₁₅Al₀.₀₅O₂ 正极的电池在文献测试中表现出更稳定的循环行为。

Nie 等 [101] 将冠醚功能化 COF（COF-Py-CE）引入含氟聚偏二氟乙烯–六氟丙烯共聚物（PVDF-HFP）基体。COF 中的正静电势区域和亚胺键，与 PVDF-HFP 中的极性 C–F 键共同作用，可以通过静电吸附和孔道限域固定 TFSI⁻。冠醚氧原子则提供了可交换的配位位点，支持 Li⁺ 在相邻 COF 层之间迁移。文献报道的 0.224 eV 对应冠醚介导的层间跳迁能垒，而不是整个复合电解质的传输活化能。在 30 °C 下，该电解质的离子电导率为 1.15 × 10⁻³ S cm⁻¹，Li⁺ 迁移数为 0.91。在所报道条件下，Li||Li 对称电池在 0.5 mA cm⁻² 和 0.5 mAh cm⁻² 条件下循环超过 1500 h。NCM523 电池在 1 C 下循环 2450 次后保持了 90% 的容量。1.2 Ah 软包电池在循环 100 次后保持了约 95.65% 的容量。

Sharon 等 [102] 比较了含 LiTFSI 的聚苯乙烯–聚环氧乙烷（SEO）嵌段共聚物电解质与 PEO 均聚物电解质。在适当组成下，SEO–LiTFSI 形成了 PEO–LiTFSI 相和聚苯乙烯纳米相周期交替的结构。富 PEO 纳米相是主要的离子传导区域，聚苯乙烯纳米相则提供力学支撑。当 Li/EO 比从 1/12 增加到 1/5 时，PEO–LiTFSI 的电导率下降了近三倍，而 SEO–LiTFSI 的电导率基本保持不变。光谱结果表明，在均聚物中，新增 Li⁺ 会占据更多 PEO 醚氧位点。在嵌段共聚物中，已配位和未配位 EO 位点的比例基本保持不变。分子动力学模拟进一步显示，缔合离子倾向于分布在纳米相界面附近。这种界面对过量盐的限域可能有助于在盐浓度增加时维持富 PEO 纳米相中的溶剂化平衡。由此，纳米相界面在该体系中可能不仅是传输障碍，也可以容纳过量盐聚集体。

综合上述液态电解质案例可以看出，自组装主要调节局部溶剂化和离子缔合。胶束网络和主–客体复合物可以改变 CIPs 和离子聚集体的比例。这些变化可能影响去溶剂化、界面层形成和电化学稳定窗口。其作用仍取决于溶剂–盐–稀释剂之间的相互作用、电极表面和运行条件。

在固态和准固态电解质中，自组装则主要组织基体内部的离子传输路径。上述案例结合了分子限域、周期性相分离和可交换配位位点。这些结构可能在限制阴离子运动的同时，为 Li⁺ 迁移保留连续路径。其传输和力学功能仍与框架化学、形貌和温度相关。Yan 等 [103] 开发了用于 PEO 基固态聚合物电解质的选择性氟化芳香锂盐（SFALS）。SFALS 阴离子与 PEO 之间的氢键作用参与形成了“三联体”型 Li⁺ 溶剂化结构。报道结果显示，该体系的 Li⁺ 配位和界面化学发生了变化，并在所报道条件下表现出稳定循环。

总体而言，自组装将电解质结构与载流子传输联系起来，作用尺度覆盖分子到介观尺度。在液态体系中，它主要重组局部溶剂化和离子缔合。在固态和准固态体系中，它可以组织长程传输路径，并使离子传输与力学支撑形成结构上的配合。这些功能并非仅由自组装本身产生，而是取决于组成、形貌、界面和运行条件。因此，下一节将进一步讨论如何把类似的设计原则从电解质体相延伸到固态电解质界面。
