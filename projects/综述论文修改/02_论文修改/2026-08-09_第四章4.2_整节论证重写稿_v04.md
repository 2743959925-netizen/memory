# 第四章 4.2 Electrolyte Architectures: From Local Solvation to Long-Range Ion Transport

## 英文整节修改稿

Liquid and solid electrolytes do not use the same transport mode. Liquid electrolytes can continuously reorganize local solvation shells and ion associations. Solid electrolytes instead require connected pathways through a mechanically constrained matrix. The role of self-assembly therefore changes with the phase. In liquids, it mainly tunes local coordination and interfacial chemistry. In solids, it must connect molecular interactions with long-range ion transport and mechanical continuity. This phase-dependent translation is the central question of this section.

Conventional ethylene carbonate/diethyl carbonate (EC/DEC) electrolytes illustrate the liquid baseline. Brownian motion allows solvation shells and the surrounding solvent to exchange continuously. In the conventional localized high-concentration electrolyte (LHCE) model, the diluent contributes little to the primary solvation shell, whereas salt–solvent clusters remain dispersed in the diluent-rich phase. This distinction matters because self-assembly in liquids primarily redistributes local chemical environments rather than constructing a solid-like transport framework.

Efaw et al. [97] and Cao et al. [98] show how this local redistribution can also shape the electrode interface. In the LiFSI/DME/TFEO system, preferential interactions between the salt, solvent, and diluent produce micelle-like salt–solvent clusters in a TFEO-rich environment. The resulting enrichment of local salt and AGG⁺ species was associated with preferential reduction and improved lithium-metal cycling. In the related study by Cao et al., TFEO altered the electronic and solvation environment and supported the formation of a LiF-rich monolithic solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI). These studies therefore connect bulk solvation organization with interphase chemistry, rather than treating electrolyte composition and SEI formation as separate design problems.

Yao et al. [99] reached a similar conclusion through a discrete host–guest structure. 15-C-5 formed [Na(15-C-5)]⁺ with Na⁺ and shifted PF₆⁻ participation toward the primary solvation shell. This change favored contact ion pairs (CIPs), reduced free-solvent coordination, and facilitated low-temperature Na⁺ transport and interfacial desolvation. Unlike the micelle-like system, the assembled unit was a molecular complex. Both strategies, however, regulate ion association by changing the local coordination environment.

Taken together, the liquid examples define the first design boundary. Self-assembly acts mainly on solvation shells, ion-pair populations, and the chemistry of the evolving interphase. It does not by itself provide a long-range pathway through a solid matrix. Solid-state design therefore requires the same molecular interactions to be embedded in architectures that maintain transport continuity.

Ding et al. [100] and Nie et al. [101] illustrate two ways to make that transition. Ding et al. used a PEG/18C6/HDIt polyrotaxane network, where crown-ether coordination and steric confinement act within a flexible three-dimensional framework. Nie et al. placed crown-ether sites in COF-Py-CE/PVDF-HFP, where pore confinement immobilizes TFSI⁻ and periodic coordination sites support Li⁺ hopping. The former emphasizes a deformable supramolecular network; the latter turns ion selectivity into a channel-architecture problem. Their reported Li⁺ transference numbers, 0.41 and 0.91, respectively, support this distinction.

Sharon et al. [102] add a different solution through SEO–PEO microphase separation. PEO-rich domains carry ions, whereas polystyrene domains provide mechanical support. The interfaces also accommodate excess salt and help preserve the solvation balance within the conducting domains. This division of functions explains why the block copolymer can retain its conductivity as salt loading increases, whereas a PEO homopolymer becomes more strongly associated and less conductive. Here, the phase boundary is not simply a transport obstacle. It is part of the transport design.

Yan et al. [103] further show that local solvation control remains relevant in a solid polymer matrix. Hydrogen bonding between SFALS anions and PEO produces a triad-like Li⁺ solvation structure. The example links a liquid-electrolyte design variable, local coordination, with a solid-state requirement, maintaining transport within a polymer framework. It therefore reinforces the need for conditional translation rather than direct transfer of liquid rules.

Across these studies, the design problem can be stated in two steps. Liquid-electrolyte self-assembly first reorganizes local solvation and ion association, then influences desolvation and interphase formation. Solid and quasi-solid electrolytes must additionally create connected Li⁺ pathways, restrict anion motion, and preserve mechanical continuity. The balance among these functions depends on matrix chemistry, morphology, interfaces, and temperature. Self-assembly is therefore better viewed as a way to match architecture with transport mode than as a universal performance enhancer.

This distinction also prepares the transition to solid-state electrolyte interfaces. Bulk electrolyte architecture determines how ions and stresses are distributed before the electrolyte meets an electrode. At the solid–solid boundary, contact continuity, interphase evolution, and local space-charge effects become decisive. The next section examines these interfacial roles.

## 中文对照

液态和固态电解质并不遵循相同的传输模式。液态电解质可以持续重组局部溶剂化壳层和离子缔合状态。固态电解质则需要在受力学约束的基体中建立连续传输路径。因此，自组装在不同相态中的作用也不相同。在液态体系中，它主要调节局部配位和界面化学。在固态体系中，它还需要把分子间作用连接到长程离子传输和力学连续性。这种具有相态差异的迁移，是本节讨论的核心。

常规碳酸乙烯酯/碳酸二乙酯（EC/DEC）电解液可以作为液态体系的参照。布朗运动使溶剂化壳层与周围溶剂持续交换。在传统局部高浓度电解液（LHCE）模型中，稀释剂对第一溶剂化壳层的贡献较小，而盐–溶剂团簇分散在富稀释剂相中。这一区别很重要，因为液态体系中的自组装主要重分配局部化学环境，而不是直接构建类似固态电解质的长程传输框架。

Efaw 等 [97] 和 Cao 等 [98] 说明，这种局部重组还可以延伸到电极界面。在 LiFSI/DME/TFEO 体系中，盐、溶剂和稀释剂之间的选择性相互作用形成了富 TFEO 环境中的类胶束盐–溶剂团簇。局部盐浓度和 AGG⁺ 物种增加，并与优先还原和锂金属循环稳定性相关。在 Cao 等的相关研究中，TFEO 改变了电解液的电子和溶剂化环境，并与富 LiF 整体式固态电解质界面（SEI）的形成相关。由此，这两项工作把体相溶剂化组织与界面层化学联系起来，而不是将电解液组成和 SEI 形成视为两个彼此独立的设计问题。

Yao 等 [99] 通过离散的主–客体结构得到了相近的认识。15-C-5 与 Na⁺ 形成 [Na(15-C-5)]⁺，并改变了 PF₆⁻ 参与第一溶剂化壳层的方式。这一变化有利于接触离子对（CIPs）的形成，减少游离溶剂配位，并促进低温 Na⁺ 传输和界面去溶剂化。与类胶束体系不同，这里的组装单元是分子复合物。但两种策略都通过改变局部配位环境调节离子缔合。

综合上述液态案例可以确定第一个设计边界。液态电解液中的自组装主要作用于溶剂化壳层、离子对比例和演化界面的化学组成。它本身并不能在固态基体中提供长程传输路径。因此，固态电解质需要把相同的分子间作用嵌入能够维持传输连续性的结构中。

Ding 等 [100] 和 Nie 等 [101] 展示了实现这一迁移的两种方式。Ding 等构建了 PEG/18C6/HDIt 聚轮烷网络，使冠醚配位和空间限域作用发生在柔性的三维框架中。Nie 等则将冠醚位点引入 COF-Py-CE/PVDF-HFP，通过孔道限域固定 TFSI⁻，并利用周期性配位位点支持 Li⁺ 跳迁。前者强调可变形的超分子网络，后者则将离子选择性转化为孔道结构问题。两者分别报道的 Li⁺ 迁移数为 0.41 和 0.91，与这一结构差异相符。

Sharon 等 [102] 通过 SEO–PEO 微相分离提出了另一种方案。富 PEO 纳米相承担离子传输，聚苯乙烯纳米相提供力学支撑。纳米相界面还可以容纳过量盐，并帮助维持传导相中的溶剂化平衡。这种功能分工解释了为什么嵌段共聚物在盐含量增加时仍能保持电导率，而 PEO 均聚物则会出现更强的离子缔合和更低的电导率。在这一体系中，相界面不只是传输障碍，也是传输结构的一部分。

Yan 等 [103] 进一步表明，局部溶剂化调控在固态聚合物基体中仍然重要。SFALS 阴离子与 PEO 之间的氢键作用形成了“三联体”型 Li⁺ 溶剂化结构。该案例把液态电解液中的局部配位变量，与固态体系中维持聚合物内部传输的要求联系起来。因此，它也说明液态规则不能直接照搬到固态体系，而需要进行条件性迁移。

综合这些研究可以将设计问题概括为两个层次。液态电解液中的自组装首先重组局部溶剂化和离子缔合，随后影响去溶剂化和界面层形成。固态和准固态电解质还需要建立连续的 Li⁺ 传输路径、限制阴离子运动并保持力学连续性。这些功能之间的平衡取决于基体化学、形貌、界面和温度。因此，自组装更适合被理解为一种使结构匹配传输模式的设计方法，而不是普遍提高性能的单一手段。

这一差异也为固态电解质界面的讨论提供了衔接。电解质体相结构决定了电解质接触电极之前离子和应力的分布方式。在固–固界面处，接触连续性、界面层演化和局部空间电荷效应将进一步成为关键因素。下一节将讨论这些界面作用。
