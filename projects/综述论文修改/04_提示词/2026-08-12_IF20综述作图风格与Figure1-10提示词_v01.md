## 三、从标杆图中提炼出的共同风格

1. **一图一判断**：标题必须是带动词的结论，不用“某某机制概览”替代作者判断。
2. **固定阅读方向**：优先使用左到右的“条件/输入 → 分子或界面事件 → 结构 → 功能/失败”语法；循环过程才使用闭环。
3. **尺度分层**：分子、纳米结构、介观网络和电极结果不能混画在同一比例尺中，需用放大框或独立面板连接。
4. **颜色编码稳定**：颜色表示环境、组分或证据状态；同一对象在十张图中不得换色。建议液态蓝、准固态青、全固态灰蓝、Li⁺ 珊瑚红、有效路径绿、失败路径暗红。
5. **箭头承担论证**：实线表示正文直接支持的过程，虚线表示条件性迁移或推断，点线框表示概念假设；不要用粗大单向箭头暗示必然成功。
6. **图内少字，图注补全**：图内只放能帮助定位的短词。机制限定、适用条件和证据边界放入图注。
7. **对照比堆积更重要**：相同对象在不同介质、不同场强或不同时间窗下并排比较，比堆放多个文献案例更清楚。
8. **保留失败分支**：顶级综述图不只画理想路径。本文尤其要保留 kinetic arrest、contact loss、screening、transport blockage 和 relaxation after field removal。
9. **风格克制**：白底、扁平矢量、少量低饱和颜色、统一线宽、无拟真材质、无装饰性阴影和无意义渐变。

## 四、全套统一前缀与负面约束

将下面的“统一前缀”放在每个逐图提示词最前面。

```text
Create an original scientific review-article schematic in a restrained Nature Reviews Materials / Chemical Reviews editorial style. Landscape, designed for a 180 mm double-column figure, clean flat vector graphics on a pure white background, generous negative space, consistent 1.5–2 pt navy outlines, rounded but precise geometry, colorblind-safe restrained palette. Use a fixed visual language across the full figure set: liquid environment blue, quasi-solid teal, all-solid slate gray, Li+ coral red, anions blue, successful or demonstrated pathways green, failure or blocked pathways muted red, conditional inference dashed gray-blue, conceptual hypothesis dotted-outline box. Each panel must follow condition/input → molecular or interfacial event → organized structure → function or failure. Separate molecular, nanoscale, mesoscale, and electrode scales with panel boundaries or zoom connectors. Keep visible text minimal; reserve clean label zones for later vector typography. No decorative 3D, no photorealism, no glossy rendering, no gradients, no shadows, no stock icons, no journal logos, no citations, no performance numbers, no invented chemical structures, no invented crystal lattices, no misleading unconditional arrows, no tiny unreadable labels.
```

通用文字策略：AI 草图只保留面板字母和不超过 2–3 个词的主标签；完整术语在矢量版中人工加入。最终字号 7.5–9 pt。

## 五、Figure 1–10 逐图提示词

### Figure 1 — Conditional translation across the liquid–solid divide

```text
Four-panel composition, a–d. Panel a spans the top and compares three adjacent environments using the same icon language. LIQUID: dispersed generic solvent symbols, highly mobile Li+ ions, free motion arrows and a fully wetted electrode surface. QUASI-SOLID: a polymer or gel network containing limited liquid, tortuous but continuous ion paths and partial mechanical constraint. ALL-SOLID: densely contacting active-material and solid-electrolyte particles, narrow solid–solid contacts, stress arrows, no free-flowing liquid. Do not use large arrows directly connecting the three environments; instead use subtle separators to prevent implying inevitable translation. Panel b shows four transferable design functions as original pictograms: molecular anchoring, layered organization, reversible local reconstruction and field-directed ordering. A dashed conditional connector leads from these functions to panel c. Panel c contains three sequential validation gates: DRIVING FORCE, ARCHITECTURE, FUNCTION. Each gate has a demonstrated green route and a blocked muted-red route. Panel d splits into SUCCESS and FAILURE. SUCCESS contains selective interphase, continuous ion pathway and adaptive conformal contact. FAILURE contains kinetic arrest, loss of solid–solid contact and transport blockage. Use generic solvent icons, not water-like molecular models. Preserve strong visual separation between evidence and hypothesis.
```

### Figure 2 — Cross-disciplinary functions of self-assembly

```text
Build a balanced cross-disciplinary comparison without copying published figures. Use four equal outer modules—BIOLOGY/BIOMATERIALS, PHOTOVOLTAICS, CATALYSIS, BATTERIES—around a central four-part functional core. Every outer module follows the identical visual grammar: simple building blocks → dominant interaction → organized structure → function. Biology: complementary molecular recognition forming an adaptive fibre or membrane. Photovoltaics: self-assembled monolayer passivating surface defects. Catalysis: ligands or porous nodes creating a localized reaction environment. Batteries: ion-selective interphase or ordered transport domain. The center lists only four short function labels: RECOGNITION, INTERFACE ORGANIZATION, DYNAMIC RECONSTRUCTION, HIERARCHICAL ORDER. Connect the shared functions toward BATTERIES with a dashed arrow labeled conceptually as transfer of design functions, while avoiding any arrow that suggests direct transfer of the original materials. Use the same molecule, interface and function icons across all four fields; no collage appearance.
```

### Figure 3 — Free-energy landscapes and kinetic states of self-assembly

```text
Three-panel scientific schematic. Panel a: a conceptual free-energy landscape with a reaction coordinate, one global minimum for equilibrium, one local minimum for a metastable state, and a deeper isolated basin separated by a high activation barrier for a kinetically trapped state. Show arrows for relaxation only when the barrier can be crossed within the observation time; include a small clock icon linking barrier height to experimental time window. Mark “conceptual, not to scale” as a reserved caption zone rather than plotted data. Panel b: a separate dissipative reaction cycle, not an endpoint on the equilibrium coordinate. Show external energy or chemical fuel entering, activation of building blocks, transient assembled state, deactivation or waste, and disassembly after input removal. Use a closed loop with a visible flux arrow. Panel c: four compact diagnostic questions represented by icons—history dependence, persistence after removing input, continuous component exchange, and relaxation within the observation window. Maintain distinct symbols for EQUILIBRIUM, METASTABLE, KINETIC TRAP and DISSIPATIVE throughout later figures.
```

### Figure 4 — Van der Waals interactions and geometry-dependent ordering

```text
Four-panel mechanism figure. Panel a: three simplified molecular pairs showing Keesom interaction between permanent dipoles, Debye interaction between a permanent dipole and an induced dipole, and London dispersion between an instantaneous dipole and an induced dipole. Use correct dipole orientation and exact spelling; do not draw covalent bonds between separate molecules. Panel b: a qualitative short-range potential curve with attraction increasing at shorter separation and steep repulsion at contact; no numerical axes or unsupported formula. Panel c: compare spherical particles with largely non-directional contacts against faceted particles whose geometry restricts contact orientation and produces ordered packing. Panel d: evaporation-assisted ZIF-8 packing as a representative process: solvent evaporation and a receding meniscus create capillary confinement while faceted geometry and short-range attraction promote ordered packing. Visually separate van der Waals attraction from capillary and shape effects so the figure does not claim that van der Waals forces alone determine order.
```

### Figure 5 — Electrostatic interactions under dielectric and ionic environments

```text
Five-panel comparison. Panel a shows free surface charge, induced polarization and an electric double layer around a particle using distinct symbols. Panel b compares the same charged particle pair in weakly versus strongly polarizable media, changing field lines and induced polarization while keeping particle charge constant. Panel c compares low and high ionic strength: thick versus compressed double layers, long versus short interaction range, and different aggregation tendency; include counterions and co-ions without inventing exact concentrations. Panel d is a layer-by-layer assembly cycle: positively charged surface → adsorption → charge reversal → negatively charged layer → repeated multilayer growth, with controlled thickness and composition indicated visually. Panel e is a small boundary note showing that isotropic electrostatic attraction can promote contact and layering, whereas precise molecular orientation usually requires directional interactions. Do not reduce electrostatic assembly to a simple “opposite charges always attract” cartoon.
```

### Figure 6 — Directional interactions across molecular and supramolecular scales

```text
Four-panel figure using a consistent scale ladder. Panel a: hydrogen-bond donor and acceptor geometry produces a reversible network; branch into an ordered hydrogen-bonded organic framework channel and a self-healing junction as two possible structural consequences, without implying they are the same material. Panel b: aromatic units compare face-to-face, slipped/offset and edge-related pi arrangements; only the compatible geometry continues to an ordered interchain packing or narrowed transport channel. Panel c: a generic metal–ligand or host–guest recognition motif forms defined nodes and links, then an ordered supramolecular structure. Panel d: a horizontal scale ladder connects intramolecular conformation → supramolecular fibre/network → mesoscale architecture → observable property. Use zoom connectors and distinct scale bars as placeholders. Show interaction cooperativity where appropriate; do not imply that one directional bond alone guarantees macroscopic function.
```

### Figure 7 — Dynamic covalent exchange pathways

```text
Five-panel chemistry-first schematic. Panel a: a generic polymer network bond enters an activated exchange state and reconnects with preserved overall covalent network connectivity; show topology rearrangement rather than network dissolution. Panel b: correct disulfide chemistry using R–S–S–R and either a reduction/oxidation pair or a thiol–disulfide exchange pathway; never draw S=S or SH–SH as the disulfide bond. Panel c: carbonyl plus primary amine reversibly forms an imine plus water, with pH and water environment shown only as condition icons. Panel d: boronic acid/diol boronate-ester formation and exchange, visually connected to the TA/CMC/BA binder chemistry used in Figure 8. Panel e: Diels–Alder association and retro-Diels–Alder dissociation only if retained in the manuscript, with different thermal condition icons. Reserve all exact chemical structures for manual vector verification; AI should produce topology placeholders rather than authoritative formulas.
```

### Figure 8 — Multiscale stress dissipation and network repair in a silicon-anode binder

```text
Five-step multiscale sequence from left to right. Panel a: initial TA/CMC/BA binder network around silicon particles. Distinguish CMC chains, TA multifunctional nodes, reversible hydrogen bonds and dynamic boronate-ester crosslinks with a compact legend. Panel b: silicon particle expansion creates local tensile stress and incipient binder damage; hydrogen bonds break first as sacrificial interactions. Panel c: broken hydrogen bonds dissipate stress while polymer chains locally slide and rearrange, reducing stress concentration. Panel d: boronate-ester exchange reconnects the network and restores local contact; do not imply instantaneous complete healing. Panel e: zoom out to retained particle–binder–conductive-network contact and electrode structural integrity. Use a top molecular-scale strip and a lower particle/electrode-scale strip connected by zoom arrows. Do not add cycling numbers, capacity values, dendrite suppression, or performance claims not directly supported by the manuscript.
```

### Figure 9 — External-field-induced assembly: coupling, morphology and persistence

```text
Three equal vertical columns for OPTICAL, ELECTRIC and MAGNETIC fields, all using the identical top-to-bottom syntax: field → coupled object → interaction or pathway change → assembled morphology → persistence after field removal. OPTICAL: trans–cis isomerization of a photoresponsive group changes hydrogen-bonded fibre organization and leads to a qualitative modulus or yield-stress change; place PET as a small dotted-outline inset marked as intramolecular conformational evidence only, not proven macroscopic assembly. ELECTRIC: induced dipoles form chains, then lateral merging or BCT/FCC–BCT transition; add a small frequency strip showing relative electric-double-layer polarization at low frequency versus bulk dielectric polarization at higher frequency, without numbers. MAGNETIC: magnetic moments align into chains or networks; a DNA-related example must show concentration, field intensity/direction and substrate as co-determinants. Along the bottom, apply the Figure 3 state language to a persistence bar with FIELD-DEPENDENT, METASTABLE AFTER REMOVAL and KINETICALLY TRAPPED. Avoid treating field application as equivalent to durable order.
```

### Figure 10 — Entropic, polymerization-induced and crystallization-driven pathways

```text
Five-panel pathway comparison. Panel a: depletion interaction. Compare two separated large particles with non-overlapping exclusion zones against approaching particles with overlapping exclusion zones; show increased free volume for small depletant particles and an osmotic imbalance, without drawing depletion as a chemical bond. Panel b: compare matched and mismatched surface curvature to show that geometry changes overlap volume and attraction. Panel c: PISA pathway—soluble macro-chain-transfer precursor, growth of an insoluble block, onset of amphiphilicity, nucleation, then sphere → worm → vesicle morphology transitions as polymerization proceeds. Make clear that chain growth and morphology evolution are coupled, not two separate operations. Panel d: CDSA pathway—crystalline seed, epitaxial addition at an active growth front, controlled one-dimensional fibre and two-dimensional platelet growth; emphasize lattice matching and seeded kinetic control. Panel e: a dotted-outline PEO-based electrolyte hypothesis box requiring validation of domain connectivity, crystallinity, segmental motion, ion transport and dendrite response. Keep depletion, PISA and CDSA at the level of evolutionary pathways, not three equivalent attractive forces.
```
